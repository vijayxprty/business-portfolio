CASE STUDY — MIDAS METALS

Spare change,
turned into gold.

Midas Metals turns spare change into real gold, silver, platinum, palladium and rhodium. Buy a coffee, round up to the dollar, and the difference goes into your metals vault. I took it from a blank page to a shipped product — research, flows, wireframes, the app for iOS and Android, and the website — then worked with the developers until what went live matched the designs.

CLIENTMidas Metals
WHAT I DIDApp UX + UI, website design + HTML / WordPress
YEAR2019

01 / THE APP

Four screens.
One journey.

From the first hello to a connected bank — the whole product in the order a new user meets it. Tap any screen to see it bigger.

02 / THE BRIEF

Gold felt like
a rich person’s game.

Vaults, dealers, minimums. The app had to make a first-time buyer feel confident in under a minute.

The problem

Precious metals are one of the oldest stores of value there is — but buying them felt like joining a club you weren’t invited to. Prices in troy ounces, dealers, storage, minimum orders. Most people never got past the first Google search.

Midas Metals removed the barriers: no minimums, no dealer, no vault to rent. Your spare change buys real metal, stored for you. My job was to make that idea feel obvious the moment someone opened the app — and safe enough to connect a bank account to.

What I did

  • UX + UI for the iOS & Android app — flows, screens and the full visual system
  • The eVault home screen — one donut, five metals, total weight in ounces
  • Round-Ups — the $5 trigger, per-metal multipliers, the $70 buy summary
  • Bank connection — search plus familiar bank tiles, built for trust
  • Marketing website — design, HTML and WordPress build
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Grey boxes first.
Colour came second.

Every key screen was sketched and tested as a wireframe before a single colour went in. The layout had to work in grey — or it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the vault is a donut, not a list — weight in ounces is the hero number, not the dollar figure. Round-ups are sliders with a multiplier, not a settings page. And bank connection leads with familiar logos, not a search box alone.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished screens.

01

The vault read in under 5 seconds

8 out of 10 first-time testers could say what they owned and what it was worth within 5 seconds of opening the home screen. The donut plus the weight in ounces did the work — a dollar figure alone never landed the same way.

02

The $5 trigger had to be on the screen

Early testers kept asking when a round-up would actually happen. Putting “Round ups are triggered when your spare change totals $5” at the top of the screen ended the question entirely. In the next round of testing, nobody asked it once.

03

Bank logos beat a list

Showing Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citi and the rest as familiar tiles got people to a connected bank about a third faster than a plain dropdown in the same test. Recognition is faster than recall — especially with money.

04

Multipliers were the most-tapped control

Letting people lock a ×3 or ×5 multiplier per metal turned a settings screen into the fun part. Platinum at ×5 was the most-picked combination in testing — people loved choosing how fast each metal grows.

Discover
