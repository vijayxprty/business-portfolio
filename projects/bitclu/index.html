CASE STUDY — BITCLU

Clarity in
every business decision.

Bitclu is a performance analytics dashboard for Amazon sellers — real-time sales, profits, ad spend and inventory alerts, built so a seller can see what matters in one glance. I led the UI/UX and the front end: Photoshop mockups turned into production HTML, CSS and Bootstrap, then integrated into AngularJS — a framework I learned from scratch on the job.

02 / THE BRIEF

Sellers were drowning
in data.

Seller Central gave them reports, Helium10 gave them more reports — but nobody could see, in one place, whether the business was actually making money. Sales up, profit down, a product quietly out of stock: the answers were spread across five tools.

The problem

Amazon sellers lived in spreadsheets and dashboards that never agreed with each other. Was a product profitable after ad spend? Which listing needed attention today? What was about to run out of stock? By the time a seller had the answer, the moment to act on it had passed.

Bitclu had to become the one place a seller opened first: real-time sales, profits, ad spend and inventory alerts, in a layout a busy seller could read in seconds. My job was the whole of it — design the product, then build the front end and integrate it into AngularJS, learning the framework from scratch as I went.

What I did

  • Dashboard overview — real-time sales, profits, ad spend, product ranking
  • Product insights — cost, sales trend and net margin per product
  • ROI & profitability — ad performance against operational cost
  • Sales trends chart — weekly and monthly comparisons
  • Inventory alerts — low-stock warnings and fulfilment lag
  • Export & report builder — customisable CSV and PDF reports
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Annotated, not guessed.
Every box earned its place.

Every key screen was sketched as an annotated wireframe before a single colour went in — each module numbered, each number explained. The layout had to justify itself in grey, or it didn't ship.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the overview answers in one glance — sales, profit, ad spend, ranking. Net margin sits next to sales on every row, so a loss never hides behind a big sales number. And red is reserved for one job: loss-making SKUs.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real sellers.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past three early Amazon sellers — informal calls, sourced from seller communities — and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished product.

01

Red means look here

Loss-making SKUs are red. Sellers didn't read the table — they scanned for red. A red margin cell meant a product was losing money after ads, and it was visible from across the room. Colour did the triage before any number did.

02

Export became the feature

I pushed for customisable CSV and PDF exports because sellers lived in spreadsheets. It became one of the most-used parts of the product — the dashboard convinced, the export closed the loop.

03

Profit first, sales second

Every product row shows net margin next to sales and ad spend. A big sales number never hides a loss — the number sellers actually asked about was "what did I keep?"

04

The morning check

The overview is the first screen: sales, profit, ad spend and ranking in one glance. Sellers checked it first thing and acted on it before the day started — so the overview had to answer in seconds, not after a scroll.
