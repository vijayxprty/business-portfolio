CASE STUDY — XCASH REWARDS

Extra benefits,
in every scan.

xCash is a Thai rewards and payments platform — scan, convert points, transfer, top up, e-vouchers and donations, with an app to match. I designed the website around one idea: every everyday payment should earn something back — and the site’s first job is to get the app onto your phone.

02 / THE BRIEF

Six ways to move money.
One brand nobody knew.

xCash does six different things — scan to pay, convert points, transfer, top up, e-vouchers, donations — and the website had to explain all of them to people who had never heard of the brand, in Thai, and get them to download the app.

The problem

A payments brand nobody recognises has two problems at once: nobody trusts it yet, and nobody understands what it does. The site had to earn trust in the first screen and make six services feel like one simple product — then hand the visitor to the app, because that is where the product actually lives.

The site is entirely in Thai, for Thai users — Thai copy, a Bangkok address, Thai support hours. Nothing on the page tries to be international, and that is exactly what makes it feel like it belongs to the people it is for.

What I did

  • Homepage + award hero — the 3rd SME Start Up Award leads the page
  • About section — the app itself, in one phone mockup
  • Six-service grid — one card, one icon per way money moves
  • App download band — Google Play + App Store, above the fold
  • How-it-works video — “just open the app and scan”
  • Contact + footer — Bangkok address, support hours, © 2023
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Pink marker first.
Pixels came second.

Every section was sketched in xCash pink before a single screenshot went in. The layout had to work as a marker sketch — or it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the award is the hero — trophy and certificate, not a product pitch. The six services live in one grid, so the whole product fits in a single glance. And the download band carries the phone, not a paragraph — the site hands you the app instead of describing it.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The award is the trust signal

The hero leads with the 3rd SME Start Up Award — trophy and certificate — not a product pitch. For a payments brand people haven’t heard of, a government-backed award does the trusting before a single word is read.

02

Six services, one grid

Scan, Convert point, Transfer, Mobile Top Up, E-Voucher, Donation. Every way money moves in the app gets one card, one icon, one line — so a first-time visitor sees the whole product in a single glance, without scrolling.

03

The app is the product, the site is the door

The download band sits high on the page with both store badges, and the phone mockup shows the real app home — Scan, Transfer, Voucher, Convert, My QR, Donate. The site doesn’t explain the app; it hands you the app.

04

One language, one audience

The whole site is in Thai, for Thai users — Thai copy, a Bangkok address, support hours 9.00–18.00. Nothing on the page tries to be international, and that is why it reads as trustworthy to the people it is for.
