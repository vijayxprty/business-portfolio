02 / THE BRIEF

Eight platforms.
Eight different experiences.

Equus is a US leader in custom computing infrastructure — servers and workstations for hyperscalers like Google and Netflix, each needing tailored hardware and high-volume ordering. When I was brought in, every platform had grown on its own. My mission: strategise, design, and unify the entire digital ecosystem across all brands and platforms.

The problem

Each platform had grown independently with its own UI, UX, and structural logic — an inconsistent brand experience, fragmented workflows across engineers, resellers and ops, inefficient internal tools (especially HUB), disconnected marketing assets and documentation, and no shared design language anywhere.

That created friction for internal users and external clients alike — reducing efficiency and trust. A reseller, an engineer and a buyer could all be looking at Equus and seeing three different companies.

What I did

  • All 8 platforms redesigned — corporate site, storefront, partner portal, HUB and more
  • One component library in Figma — shared across every platform, for faster handoff
  • Research first — stakeholder interviews, Google Analytics, Hotjar, internal ticket logs
  • RICE prioritisation + service blueprints + journey maps — the structure decided on paper
  • 20+ PDFs, spec sheets and diagrams — designed inside the same system
  • WCAG-compliant, fully responsive — every platform, every screen size

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Measured first.
Designed second.

Before the redesign, I ran the numbers — Google Analytics, Hotjar, internal ticket logs — and talked to the people who lived in these tools every day. Four findings shaped everything that followed.

01

The bounce was a wayfinding problem

Product pages were losing 18–20% of visitors. The specs were there — but unclear documentation and spec visuals meant buyers could not find the right configuration fast enough, so they left.

02

HUB leaked a third of its users mid-flow

Drop-off in the middle of the HUB funnel was running at around 30%. The multi-step approval flow asked operators to re-enter information the system already had, and forced redundant approvals that could have been auto-resolved.

03

Support tickets were the symptom

Internal support logs kept coming back to the same thing: UI confusion and miscommunication. The interface was generating the support burden — so fixing the interface was the support fix.

04

One system, not eight redesigns

The fix was never eight separate redesigns. One design language, one component library, one way of working — applied across the corporate site, the storefront, the partner portal and HUB, so every platform felt like the same company.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
