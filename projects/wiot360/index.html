CASE STUDY — WIOT360

Designing at
IoT scale.

02 / THE BRIEF

One school-bus product.
Twenty-one services to become.

WIOT360 started as school-bus safety. The client wanted it to become a full-suite ecosystem — governments, logistics firms, schools and city transit — without losing the trust it had built with parents.

The problem

WIOT360 was a single-use transportation platform. The client wanted to transform it into a multi-sector, cross-device ecosystem — and the design had to carry that whole jump: a modular architecture that could stretch across 21+ services, a data-rich visual language for dashboards, and end-user apps that felt simple to parents, drivers and transport managers alike.

Underneath it all, the system had to be geo-fenced and AI-driven, communicating real-time alerts and decisions — arrivals, departures, zone breaches — to the right person at the right second, while still looking like a brand you would trust with your child’s journey to school.

What I did

  • All 21 services, designed — every service and its dashboard, from one system
  • Led the offshore design team — the client’s internal team, executing to my direction
  • Full UX strategy — research to deployment, across sprints
  • 3 mobile apps + 7 dashboards + marketing site — parent, driver and admin apps
  • A/B testing + usability loops — Hotjar, analytics funnels, remote testing
  • Wireframes to design systems — lo-fi and hi-fi prototypes, component libraries, brand extensions
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Dot-grid first.
Live data came second.

Every dashboard and app flow was laid out on a dot grid before a single real data point went in. The layout had to hold together with boxes and lines — or it would never survive live fleet data.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the dashboard leads with the live map, not a table — a transport manager sees the network before the numbers. The driver app is one thumb’s work: trip, fuel, checklist, done. And Pax & Seats pairs the seat map with the roster on the same screen, so nobody is counted twice.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Three audiences.
One system, three jobs.

Parents, drivers and transport managers all live in the same ecosystem — but they open it for completely different reasons. Four findings shaped the finished platform.

01

Three audiences, three apps

Parents want to know where the bus is. Drivers want their shift, fuel log and checklist. Transport managers want the whole fleet at a glance. One shared system couldn’t serve all three — so each got their own app, built on the same design system, with the same data underneath.

02

Alerts are the product

Arrival, departure, zone breach, geofence breach — the alerts are why people trust the platform. The design had to surface them the second they happened, in the parent app, the admin app and on the dashboard, with no digging through menus first.

03

Tested in loops, not once

Hotjar session recordings and heatmaps, analytics funnel tracking, feedback surveys in staging builds, and remote usability testing over Lookback and Google Meet. In post-launch surveys and heatmap sessions, onboarding confusion dropped 37% — and key tasks like trip logging, location tracking and report generation hit a 90% completion rate.

04

Modular by contract

Each dashboard is modular — services toggle on or off based on the client’s service contract. One design system stretches from a school-bus dashboard to a jet-ski dispatch board to garbage-truck monitoring, without a redesign for every new client.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
