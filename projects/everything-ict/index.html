02 / THE BRIEF

Buying school ICT meant
phone calls and guesswork.

Most school buyers aren’t procurement experts — yet every purchase has to be compliant, good value, and right for the classroom. The site had to make a framework purchase feel as easy as shopping, without hiding the compliance that makes it safe.

The problem

Buying ICT for a school or a trust meant phone calls, spreadsheets and hoping the purchase was compliant. Which framework? Which supplier? Is the price fair, and will the kit actually suit a classroom? Most buyers gave up on comparing and just rang whoever they knew.

Everything ICT removes the friction: one menu carries the whole catalogue, the homepage meets buyers at their level of confidence, and a real person is always one call away. My job was to make a compliant purchase feel obvious from the first screen — for a headteacher buying ten laptops and a trust buying for fifty schools alike.

What I did

  • Mega menu — eight categories, every sub-item one hover away
  • Homepage hero — the promise in one line, two clear next steps
  • Three doors in — know what you need, check you’re compliant, or new to procurement
  • Proof sections — savings, reviews, testimonials and the trusts themselves
  • Contact form that asks the category first — so the reply starts useful
  • Footer as a second menu — every route back into the site
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Marker first.
Pixels came second.

Every key screen was sketched on a whiteboard before a single colour went in — marker lines, boxes and arrows. If the layout didn’t read in marker, it wouldn’t read in pixels either.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the menu carries the catalogue, so the homepage doesn’t have to. Buyers arrive at three different levels of confidence, so the page gives them three doors, not one. And the proof — savings, reviews, real schools — comes before the form, never after it.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time buyers and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The menu is the product

School buyers came looking for a category, not a homepage. In testing, they found Laptops or AV screens from the menu before reading a single line of body copy — so the menu carries the whole catalogue in one hover, and the homepage is free to sell the promise instead of listing products.

02

Three doors, not one pitch

Buyers arrived at three levels of confidence: sure what they needed, unsure they were compliant, or completely new to procurement. One pitch confused all three — the “three ways to move forward” cards let each buyer pick their own door in seconds, and the bounce rate on the hero fell away.

03

People, not portals

The page says it plainly — “Procurement powered by people, not portals” — and the testimonials carry real names and roles. In testing, buyers trusted a named person far more than a portal login, so the page puts faces and quotes ahead of the form, and the form asks for the category first so the reply starts useful.

04

Proof before the ask

£5M+ in public sector savings, 87+ five-star reviews, 6,000+ schools, 76+ trusted suppliers — the numbers sit above the contact form, never below it. In testing, nobody asked “can I trust these people?” once the proof was on the page; they asked what to buy next.
