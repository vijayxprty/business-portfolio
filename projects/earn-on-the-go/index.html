CASE STUDY — EARN ON THE GO

Listen. Answer.
Earn.

Forbeto pays you to listen. Sponsored audio plays, you answer 2 out of 3 questions right, and the money lands in your balance — redeem it through PayPal or donate it to charity. I designed the whole loop end to end: the playlist, the questions, the directory of local businesses, the balance and the payout — 31 screens in Adobe XD, for iOS and Android.

02 / THE BRIEF

Banners get ignored.
Listening gets paid.

Local businesses buy attention and get skipped. Forbeto flips it: the listener gets paid to actually listen, and the business only pays for attention proven with answers.

The problem

Local businesses spend real money on ads that nobody notices. Banner ads scroll past, radio spots play to empty rooms — and the business has no way to know if anyone heard a word.

Forbeto made attention measurable: you listen to a sponsored audio message, answer three quick questions about it, and get paid. The business knows its message landed, because the listener proved it.

What I did

  • UX + UI for the iOS & Android app — 31 screens in Adobe XD, splash to PayPal
  • The listen–answer–earn loop — playlist, audio cards, and the 3-question check
  • Balance & redemption — PayPal payout and charity donation
  • Business directory — local sponsors with distance and one-tap actions
  • Onboarding that stays out of the way — mobile login, zip code only at redemption
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Green marker first.
Pixels came second.

Every key screen was sketched as a wireframe before a single colour went in. The layout had to work in green marker — or it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the reward is on the card, not hidden in a menu — every listen shows its dollar value. Verification waits until redemption, so sign-up is one field and one button. And the balance screen is the home of the app’s promise — the money is always visible, never buried.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished screens.

01

2 out of 3 was the magic number

One wrong answer shouldn’t end the reward. The playlist promises credit for 2 out of 3 correct — forgiving enough to keep people listening, strict enough that the sponsor’s message actually lands.

02

The money has to be visible

Every audio card carries its price — $1.50 in progress, $2.00 available — and the balance screen leads with $60. People press play when they can see what a listen is worth.

03

Verify at the payout, not the sign-up

Zip code verification only appears when you redeem. Asking for it at login would have cost sign-ups; asking at the moment of value makes it feel fair.

04

The directory is the second product

Super Bagels at 4.5 miles, Bevy Fashion Hub at 5.5 — the same app that pays you to listen also shows you the local businesses behind the audio. The sponsors get discovered twice.
