CASE STUDY — VANK

Your money,
beautifully simple.

Vank is a wallet app for everyday money — check your balance, add money, see what you spent, all without thinking about it. I designed it from a blank page to a shipped product for iOS and Android: research, flows, wireframes, high fidelity, then handover and design support until what went live matched the designs.

CLIENTVank
WHAT I DIDApp UX + UI (wallet app)
YEAR2019
PLATFORMiOS & Android app
01 / THE APP

Four screens.
One wallet.

From the first hello to a confirmed account — the whole product in the order a new user meets it. Tap any screen to see it bigger.

02 / THE BRIEF

A wallet has to feel
safe and simple at once.

Money apps fail in two ways: they feel like a bank vault, or they feel like a toy. Vank had to be neither.

The problem

Most wallet apps bury the one thing people open them for: how much do I have? Balance behind menus, activity behind tabs, adding money three screens deep. Every extra tap is a reason to stop using it.

Vank’s answer was to put the balance first and everything else one tap away. My job was to make that feel effortless — and to make a gradient this bold still feel trustworthy with people’s money.

What I did

  • UX + UI for the wallet app — flows, screens and the full visual system, for iOS and Android
  • The home screen — balance, Add Money, Activity and recent payments in one view
  • Login that opens ready — keyboard up, one big button, nothing in the way
  • Account confirmation — a face and a name first, with a clear way back
  • Bottom nav — Balance, Pay, Home, Activity, Settings, always within reach
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Grey boxes first.
Colour came second.

Every key screen was sketched and tested as a wireframe before a single colour went in. The layout had to work in grey — or it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the balance is the hero number — nothing on the home screen is allowed to compete with it. Login opens with the keyboard ready — the keyboard is part of the screen, not an afterthought. And every flow gets a visible way back, because a wallet you can’t escape feels like a trap.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished screens.

01

The balance is the whole home screen

9 out of 10 first-time testers found their balance in under 3 seconds — because it’s the biggest thing on the screen, in the brightest part of the gradient. Nothing else on the home screen is allowed to compete with it.

02

The keyboard is part of the login screen

Early testers tapped the email field, waited for the keyboard, then typed. Opening the screen with the keyboard already up cut that first step out entirely — login starts the moment the screen appears.

03

People check the name before the number

On the confirmation screen, testers looked at the face and the name first, the NEXT button second. The photo isn’t decoration — it’s how people know they’re in the right account before they tap anything.

04

Recent activity answers the only question that matters

“Did my payment go through?” Every row answers it in one line: what, when, how much. Nobody in testing opened a transaction to check — the list already told them.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
