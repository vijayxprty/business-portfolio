02 / THE BRIEF

A hospital at 2 a.m. needs a read.
The site has to say yes, fast.

Vesta’s customers — imaging centers, rural hospitals, urgent care clinics — need radiology coverage when their own radiologists aren’t there. The website had to make a complex, clinical service feel simple and trustworthy in one scroll.

The problem

Remote radiology is a hard thing to explain. Nighthawk reads, subspecialty interpretations, overflow support, HL7 integration, AI-assisted prioritisation — a facility manager doesn’t want a lecture, they want to know: do you cover what we need, when we need it, and can we trust you with our patients’ scans?

The old way of answering that was a wall of text. My job was to turn the service into a page a busy administrator could scan in a minute — the offer first, the proof underneath, and a way to ask for a quote without hunting for it.

What I did

  • Homepage + three service doors — Teleradiology, Telemedicine and IT Services, right under the offer
  • Services at a glance — the whole offer in nine short lines, no paragraph required
  • Nine modalities in one grid — CT to emergency coverage, each with its own card
  • AI-assisted workflow section — radiologist-led, three columns, no jargon
  • Services grid + video + blog — every service one tile, then proof in motion
  • Trust, then the ask — testimonial, Joint Commission accreditation, a decade of excellence, then the contact form
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Drawn dark.
Built for a clinical eye.

Every key section was sketched as a dark wireframe before a single colour went in — deep navy, light lines, the way a radiologist’s workstation looks. If the layout didn’t work in the dark, it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the offer is the hero — coverage, nighthawk reads, subspecialty interpretations — not a stock photo. The nine modalities live in one grid, so a facility finds its own in seconds. And trust comes before the form — testimonial and accreditation first, then the ask.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the page past first-time visitors and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished homepage.

01

Three doors, not one long pitch

Teleradiology, Telemedicine and IT Services sit as three cards directly under the opening line. In testing, visitors picked their service in seconds instead of scrolling for it — the page stopped being a brochure and started being a front desk.

02

The checklist does the selling

Services at a Glance says the whole offer in nine short lines — cost effective, no minimum reads, 24×7×365 support. Nobody had to read a paragraph to know what Vesta does, and the Request a Quote button sits right underneath it.

03

Nine modalities, one grid

CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, mammography, nuclear medicine, musculoskeletal, neuroradiology and emergency coverage in a single scannable grid. A facility manager finds their modality without opening a menu — and the grid makes the breadth of coverage obvious at a glance.

04

Trust lives at the bottom

The testimonial, the Joint Commission accreditation and “Over a Decade of Excellence” sit where a buyer looks before reaching out. The contact form then asks for facility type first — so every enquiry arrives with context instead of a cold “tell us more”.
