CASE STUDY — FAMILIASIN

Schools, families,
one place.

FamiliasIn is a school platform for supervisors — log in, see every school at a glance, and open a school to see its students and guardians. I designed it end to end, in Spanish, from a blank page to a shipped product — then stayed with the developers until what went live matched the designs.

02 / THE BRIEF

One supervisor, 150 schools,
and no single place to see them.

Supervisors were working from spreadsheets and phone calls. Which schools need attention first? Who do you call? There was no single place to see all of it — so the platform had to become that place.

The problem

A supervisor looking after schools across Uruguay was working from spreadsheets, messages and phone calls. There was no one view of the network: no way to compare schools, no way to see which ones were struggling, and no quick route from a school to the families behind it.

FamiliasIn had to be the supervisor’s desk. Log in, see every school, spot the ones that need you, and open a school to reach its students and guardians — in Spanish, because that is the language the work happens in.

What I did

  • Supervisor login — one card, two fields, one clear way in
  • School list with scores — name, puntaje, department and address in one table
  • Filters, search and export — find a school fast, take the data with you
  • School profiles — students and guardians, one click from the list
  • Four-section sidebar — Mensajes, Lista, Mapa and Configuración, always in reach
  • Spanish throughout — every label and button in the supervisor’s own language
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Drawn in blueprint.
Built in colour.

Every key screen was sketched as a blueprint wireframe before a single colour went in. If the layout didn’t work in white lines on blue, it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the login is one card, not a page of links. The list leads with the score, not the address. And a school opens into its people — students and guardians — not into more settings.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The score is the whole page

Supervisors didn’t read the table — they scanned the Puntaje column. Green meant fine, amber meant watch it, red meant call today. So the score sits in its own column, big and colour-coded, and everything else in the row stays quiet. In testing, supervisors found the weakest school on the page in under five seconds.

02

One list, not fifteen spreadsheets

The first version split schools by department. Supervisors hated it — they wanted the whole network in front of them and a filter when they needed one. So the list holds all 150 schools, ten to a page, with filters and a database export for the days they do want a spreadsheet.

03

The supervisor is the user

The login doesn’t say “welcome back” — it says “¡Bienvenido, Supervisor!”. The whole product is built around one role, so every screen assumes it: the sidebar is four sections, not forty, and the first thing you see after logging in is the list, not a dashboard of charts.

04

Spanish first, everywhere

Every label, every button, every empty state is in Spanish — Lista de Escuelas, Exportar Base de datos, Crear Cuenta. In testing, supervisors never once asked what a button meant. A platform for Uruguayan schools that speaks English would have been a platform they had to translate in their heads.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
