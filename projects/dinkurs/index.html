CASE STUDY — DINKURS

Every participant,
in one place.

Din Kurs is a Swedish participant-management system for courses, events, meetings and competitions — registrations, payments and participant lists in one flow, so organisers spend their time on the event, not the admin. I designed the website end to end, from a blank page to a shipped site, then stayed with the developers until what went live matched the designs.

02 / THE BRIEF

Courses meant spreadsheets.
Din Kurs means one flow.

Before Din Kurs, running a course meant a spreadsheet for registrations, an inbox for payments, and a separate list for participants. Double registrations slipped through, confirmations went out late, and organisers spent their evenings on admin instead of the event.

The problem

Organisers ran courses on spreadsheets, emails and separate payment services. Registration, payment and participant lists lived in different places — so double registrations slipped through, confirmations went out late, and the evening before the event was spent on admin.

Din Kurs puts it all in one flow: the participant registers, pays by card, Swish or invoice, and the organiser sees the status in real time. My job was to make that feel obvious from the first screen.

What I did

  • Homepage + hero — one clear promise: courses, events, meetings, competitions
  • Four feature cards — Participant, Payments, Reports, Event management
  • About + pricing on the same page — the system explained, then the cost, in the open
  • Product news + nine guides — the knowledge bank, kept current (November 2026)
  • FAQ + demo CTA — five answers, then “Ready to test your course?”
  • Participant app article — booking and purchasing directly on the mobile phone
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Drawn in the brand’s colours.
Teal, pink, and nothing else.

The wireframes were drawn in Din Kurs’ own teal and pink, so the structure and the brand were tested together. If a section didn’t work in two colours, it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the hero is the promise, not the product. Features are four cards, not a list. And pricing sits on the homepage, not behind a sales call.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The hero is the promise

First-time visitors read the headline before the navigation. “Easy participant management for courses, events, meetings and competitions” says what the system does in one breath — so the hero leads with the promise and the illustration shows the flow, not a product screenshot.

02

Four cards, not a list

In testing, organisers scanned for their own job first: collecting registrations, taking payment, seeing reports, managing events. Four coloured cards let each organiser find their task in seconds — a feature list made them read everything.

03

Price on the page, not behind a call

Cost was the first question every organiser asked. “From 5 SEK / participant” sits in the open on the homepage, with branding at 250 SEK a month — so the money conversation happens on the page instead of on a sales call.

04

The knowledge bank does the selling

Product news and nine guides answer the questions organisers actually ask — Swish payments, vouchers, dog courses, notification systems. In testing, the people who read a guide were the ones who booked the demo.
