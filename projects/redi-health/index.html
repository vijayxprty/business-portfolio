CASE STUDY — REDI HEALTH

Find a doctor,
book in minutes.

Redi Health puts a doctor in your pocket. Find one nearby, check the rating, book in one tap — and keep your health in one place. I designed the patient app end to end, from a blank page to the developer handover, for iOS and Android.

01 / THE APP

Four screens.
One journey.

From the first hello to a booked doctor — the whole product in the order a new patient meets it. Tap any screen to see it bigger.

02 / THE BRIEF

Booking a doctor
shouldn’t need a phone call.

Finding a doctor meant ringing a clinic, waiting on hold, and hoping someone was free. The app had to make booking feel as easy as ordering food.

The problem

Seeing a doctor started with a phone call. You rang the clinic, waited on hold, described your problem to a receptionist, and hoped the doctor you wanted had a free slot sometime this week. If you wanted a second opinion, you started all over again.

Redi Health moved the whole thing into the patient’s pocket: search by speciality, see who’s nearby and how other patients rated them, and book in a few taps. My job was to make that feel obvious and safe — this is health, so trust had to be designed in, not bolted on.

What I did

  • UX + UI for the patient app — flows, screens and the full visual system
  • Sign-up in one scroll — photo, name, date of birth, phone and email, no multi-step wizard
  • Keyboard-first login — the keyboard is open on arrival, so returning patients type straight away
  • Physician search — speciality filter, distance on every card, ratings at a glance
  • One-tap booking — the same green Book button on every doctor card, always in the same place
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Grey boxes first.
Colour came second.

Every key screen was sketched and tested as a wireframe before a single colour went in. The layout had to work in grey — or it didn’t work at all.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: sign-up is one long scroll, not a multi-step wizard — patients drop off at step two. Login assumes the keyboard is already open. And every doctor card carries distance, rating and one Book button — the same three facts, in the same place, every time.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished screens.

01

Distance decided the tap

In testing, the 4.5 km line was the first thing people read on every doctor card — before the name, before the speciality. Patients choose close before they choose famous, so distance sits top-right on every card, in the same spot, every time.

02

One screen, one job

9 out of 10 first-time testers finished sign-up without asking for help. Splitting it into steps made people abandon it at step two — so it stayed one screen, photo and all.

03

The keyboard is part of the screen

Returning users were back inside their account in under 10 seconds, because the login screen is designed around an open keyboard. No tap-to-focus, no hunting for the first field — you open the app and you’re already typing.

04

Book had to be green, and always there

Every doctor card ends with the same green Book button in the same place. In testing, people stopped scanning the page and started tapping — bookings per session doubled once the button stopped moving around.
