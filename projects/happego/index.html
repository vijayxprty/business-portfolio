CASE STUDY — HAPPEGO

Small habits,
big days.

Happego is a habit and wellness app built on a simple idea — flash the right image at the right moment, and your unconscious mind does the heavy lifting. The landing page has one job: getting someone to try the app. So everything on it earns its place — the badges, the three benefits, the film, the twelve goals — and nothing else made the cut. I designed it end to end, from a blank page to a shipped site.

02 / THE BRIEF

One page, one job.
Get the download.

A habit app lives or dies on the first download. Happego’s science — psychological priming, flashing images to nudge unconscious habits — is powerful, but hard to explain in a sentence. The page had to get a curious visitor to try the app without making them read a textbook first.

The problem

Priming works in the background: an image flashes for about a second, and your unconscious mind picks it up. Try explaining that in a headline and eyes glaze over. But bury it in paragraphs and nobody reaches the download button. The page had to make a strange idea feel simple — and make trying it feel easier than reading about it.

The answer was to let the page do the work in layers: a hero that sells the download in one glance, three plain benefits, a 39-second film for the curious, twelve goals for everyone else — and a single email field for the ones who need a night to think it over.

What I did

  • Hero that sells the download — store badges on the illustration, above the fold
  • Three plain benefits — Seamless, Customization, Versatility, one line each
  • How It Works with a 39-second film — the mechanism shown, not described
  • Twelve goal tiles — one door per life area, from Mind to Money
  • Subscribe box + social links — a way back for the not-yet-ready
  • Footer with the science — About, Benefits, The Science, Blog, Contact
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Stuck on the wall first.
Built for real second.

Every section started as a mood-board wireframe — pastel notes pinned to a board, moved around until the order felt right. The layout had to work as paper notes before it worked as a page.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the store badges sit on the hero, not below it. Three benefits, not a feature list. And the close is one email field — no form, no friction.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the page past first-time visitors and watched where they stopped, scrolled, and clicked. Four findings shaped the finished page.

01

The badges are the hero

Visitors who came to try the app never scrolled past the hero — so the Google Play and App Store badges went on the illustration itself, above everything else. The headline sets the mood; the badges do the job.

02

Three reasons, not thirty

Seamless, Customization, Versatility — one line each. The science runs deep, but the page sells three plain benefits first. The full story lives one click away on The Science page, for the people who actually want it.

03

The film does the explaining

Priming is hard to explain in words, so How It Works leads with a 39-second film instead of paragraphs. The video shows the flash-and-go mechanism in half a minute, and the text beside it is five short lines — nothing more.

04

Twelve doors into one habit

The Goals grid gives every visitor a door that sounds like them — Mind, Body, Money, Compassion. In testing, people clicked the goal that matched their own life first, then read the rest. One grid, twelve starting points.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
