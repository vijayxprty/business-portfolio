CASE STUDY — ELSEWHERE

Instant digital capacity.

Elsewhere is a Malmö custom software development company — a team of digital professionals available for swift start-up. I designed the website to make hiring a whole development team feel as simple as hiring one person: the offer in one headline, five services in one row, and the work to prove it.

02 / THE BRIEF

Deep expertise,
hard to hire at a glance.

Elsewhere could build almost anything — but a hiring manager landing on the site couldn’t see what they’d get, how the engagement worked, or who had trusted them before. The site had to turn a consultancy into a clear, hireable offer.

The problem

Elsewhere’s work was strong and varied — healthcare platforms, marine systems, event tools — but the story was buried. A visitor had to read paragraphs to learn what the company did, and nothing on the page answered the two questions every hiring manager asks first: what exactly can you do for me, and how does working with you actually work?

The redesign had to do the selling in the first screen: one headline that names the offer, five services a visitor can scan in seconds, and a process so simple it fits in three steps — then the portfolio, so the claims have proof behind them.

What I did

  • Homepage that leads with the offer — “Instant Digital Capacity”, nothing before it
  • News cards for Company & Strategy — the move to Spaces, the smartData partnership, why outsource
  • Five services as one scannable row — web, mobile, consulting, project management, UX
  • A three-step approach — Visioning, Producing, Expanding, illustrated
  • Projects portfolio with real client work — healthcare, marine, events
  • Hiring CTA + footer — contact details and the Malmö address, front and centre
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Drawn in chalk.
Built in blue.

Before the blue went in, the layout was sketched on a dark board — headline, services, portfolio — so the structure had to hold up with nothing but boxes and lines.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the headline is the offer — no paragraph before it. The five services sit in one scannable row, not a dropdown. And the portfolio comes before the contact band, so the work vouches for the pitch.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time visitors and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The headline does the selling

“Instant Digital Capacity” says what you get in three words — a team, now. In testing, visitors repeated the headline back when asked what Elsewhere does, before they’d read a single paragraph. So the hero carries the offer and nothing else; the explanation comes after the point is made.

02

Three doors into the same company

The blue, grey and teal cards — the move to Spaces, the smartData partnership, why outsource — let a visitor self-select. In testing, people picked the card that matched their own situation and ignored the rest, so the news section stays as three separate doors instead of one company update.

03

Five services, one line each

Web, mobile, consulting, project management, UX — five circles in one row. In testing, a hiring manager could name all five services after a five-second glance, because each one is a single label under a single circle. Nothing hides behind a menu.

04

The approach is the pitch

Visioning, Producing, Expanding — three illustrated steps that sell the process, not just the people. In testing, the approach section is where visitors stopped scrolling and started reading, because it answered the question the headline raises: how does working with you actually work?

From a blank page

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
