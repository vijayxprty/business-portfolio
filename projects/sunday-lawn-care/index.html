CASE STUDY — SUNDAY LAWN CARE

Personalized lawn care,
reimagined.

Sunday is a revolutionary direct-to-consumer brand delivering personalized, environmentally responsible lawn care plans — built on scientific data and user-specific insights. I designed the marketing site: homepage, landing pages, promotional sections and the visual system behind them, plus supporting social and email graphics. The goal was never just a prettier site — it was to turn scientific, personalised lawn care into something a first-time homeowner could understand and buy.

02 / THE BRIEF

Great science.
A site that didn’t sell it.

Sunday had the product right — personalised plans, real soil science, an eco mission. The site didn’t. It buried the value under jargon and a muddled page, so first-time homeowners bounced before they ever understood what they were buying.

The problem

Sunday Lawn Care is a revolutionary DTC brand with a booming presence in the U.S. and a mission rooted in sustainability. But the digital platform wasn’t carrying its weight: it needed to convey trust, precision and lifestyle appeal — while educating customers and converting visits into subscriptions. The old site had an inconsistent visual hierarchy, poor above-the-fold engagement, no clear narrative flow, and fragmented call-to-action patterns. A first-time homeowner landed, got confused, and left.

The redesign had to do three things at once: explain science-backed personalization to a general audience, speak to everyone from lawn newbies to enthusiasts, and keep the eco-consciousness at the core without losing the sale.

What I did

  • Homepage + landing pages — a clear Problem > Solution > Proof > CTA scroll flow
  • Plan personalization flow — an onboarding quiz that builds confidence instead of jargon
  • Product education sections — soil science and comparisons, in plain language
  • Trust markers — pet-safe messaging, badges, testimonials and environmental proof points
  • Visual system — natural tones, clean whitespace, friendly iconography, lifestyle photography
  • Social + email graphics — the same system, extended beyond the site
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Lo-fi first.
Stakeholders could see it in a day.

Before jumping into high fidelity, I mapped user flows and wireframes in Balsamiq. They were low-fidelity on purpose — rough enough that stakeholders reacted to the structure, not the styling, and fast enough to change in the same meeting.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the homepage tells a story in order — problem, solution, proof, then the call to action. The quiz does the personalising, so the page never has to. And trust markers sit next to the price, not buried below it.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Three real conversations.
Three different pages.

I ran semi-structured interviews, 30–40 minutes each, with three real users recruited from a Facebook gardening group, Reddit’s r/lawncare, and Sunday’s own newsletter. Three personas came out of them — and each one changed a specific design decision.

01

The audit said it first

Before any design, I audited the existing site and content. Four problems, in plain words: an inconsistent visual hierarchy, poor above-the-fold engagement, no clear narrative flow, and fragmented call-to-action patterns. The redesign started by fixing those four — not by adding decoration.

02

The gap was personalization

I reviewed TruGreen, LawnLove and GreenPal. None of them personalised the way Sunday does — and most lacked sustainability-focused visuals or any real educational content. That gap became the site’s spine: your yard, your plan, explained in plain language.

03

Three people, three decisions

Jennifer, the eco-conscious parent, distrusts “organic” claims — so the site leads with clear pet-safe messaging, trust badges and testimonials from parents. Kyle, the first-time homeowner, is overwhelmed by jargon — so he gets the onboarding quiz and progressive disclosure. Brian, the enthusiast, wants transparent data — so he gets detailed comparisons, soil testing info and downloadable PDFs.

04

Tested, not guessed

With the analytics team and the product marketing lead, we A/B tested the hero CTAs — “Start My Plan” against “Get a Free Soil Test” — plus section ordering and button placements, using InVision prototypes and Google Optimize. The launch version is the one that earned its place.

05 / HOW I WORK

From a blank page
to a shipped product.

I don’t just design screens. Every project runs through the same four phases — and I stay with it until what ships matches what was designed.

01

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
