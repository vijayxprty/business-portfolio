CASE STUDY — GRAPHIC KITCHEN

Bums on seats,
heads in beds.

Graphic Kitchen is a London hospitality design studio — packaging, menus, branding and interiors for restaurants, hotels and bars. The site has one job: make a venue owner feel this studio will fill their rooms. I designed the website end to end, from a blank page to a shipped site, then stayed with the developers until what went live matched the designs.

02 / THE BRIEF

A venue owner has one question:
will this fill my rooms?

A restaurant, hotel or bar owner doesn’t buy design — they buy full tables and booked rooms. But most design studios show pretty pictures and leave the owner guessing what it does for their business. Graphic Kitchen’s site had to do the selling: show the work, name the clients, prove the reviews — and make the call feel easy.

The problem

A venue owner choosing a design studio sees the same thing everywhere: a grid of pretty pictures and a paragraph about passion. Nothing says whether the studio understands hospitality — or whether it will make a difference to covers and bookings.

Graphic Kitchen already had the proof — big-name clients, five-star reviews, years of hospitality work. My job was to put that proof on the homepage, in the order a venue owner asks for it: what do you do, who trusts you, and how do I reach you.

What I did

  • Hero that sells the outcome — “Need more bums on seats & heads in beds”, not a studio mission statement
  • Work first — one giant WORK and a client wall of names owners recognise
  • Services as plain headlines — Packaging Design, Menu Design & Architecture, each explained in plain words
  • Reviews on the page — Google 5.0, 43 reviews, clients named by name
  • Social proof that never goes stale — the Instagram feed closes the page with fresh work
03 / FROM WIREFRAME TO SCREEN

Gold first.
Everything else follows.

The site’s whole personality is one idea: giant gold type on cream and charcoal. So the wireframes were drawn the same way — gold lines on dark cards, so the hierarchy had to work in the brand’s own colours before a single photo went in.

The wireframes settled three decisions early: the headline is the hero — gold, giant, and the first thing you read. The work comes before the services — proof, then pitch. And the reviews sit on the page, not on a separate page — trust has to be visible where the decision happens.

04 / WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

Tested with real people.
Changed by what they did.

Before the final UI, I ran the flows past first-time users and watched where they hesitated. Four findings shaped the finished pages.

01

The headline is the pitch

In testing, first-time visitors read the gold headline out loud before anything else — “bums on seats, heads in beds” told them what the studio does in five seconds. So the headline got bigger, not smaller, and the mission statement went in the bin.

02

Logos beat paragraphs

Visitors skimmed past the intro copy but stopped at the client wall. Krave, Moxy, Hotel Indigo, Jumeirah — names they recognised did more than any paragraph about the studio. So the logos moved up, right under WORK.

03

Reviews close the deal

The 5.0 Google rating was the thing people pointed at when asked what made them trust the studio. So the reviews stay on the homepage, with real names and real businesses, instead of hiding on a testimonials page.

04

The phone number never hides

Every test user who wanted to enquire looked for a phone number first, not a form. So 020 4634 0203 sits in the top bar of every section, and “Let’s Talk” never scrolls away.

Discover

Research first. Stakeholder calls, market and competitor analysis, and real user conversations — so the design solves the right problem, not the loudest one.

02

Define

Personas, journeys and flows. The structure gets decided here, on paper, before anything visual exists.

03

Design

Wireframes first, then high fidelity. Low-fi wireframes tested early, then pixel-perfect UI — validated with A/B tests, user testing and usability sessions.

04

Deliver

Handover is not the end. Annotated designs, walkthroughs with the developers, build reviews against the designs — and continuous support after launch.
